# Campaign Planning

## 1. Objective
- One primary business objective with a measurable target and deadline (for example "400 qualified demo requests by 31 March").
- Supporting KPIs per funnel stage: reach, engagement, conversion, retention. Note the current baseline for each.

## 2. Audience and positioning
- Primary segment (and secondary if any): needs, objections, where they spend attention.
- Positioning: for [audience] who [need], [product] is the [category] that [key benefit], unlike [alternative].
- Core message plus two or three proof points. Keep them consistent across channels.

## 3. Channels and content
| Channel | Role in funnel | Content | Owner | Budget | KPI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
- Choose channels by where the audience is and by the objective, not by habit. Owned (email, blog, site), earned (PR, community, partners), paid (search, social, display).
- Plan reuse: one hero asset broken into channel-specific pieces.

## 4. Timeline
- Phases: pre-launch (teasers, waitlist, press prep), launch, sustain, and wrap-up with review.
- A content calendar with dates, owners, approval steps and dependencies (legal review, design, product readiness).

## 5. Measurement
- UTM convention for every link: `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` (lowercase, consistent).
- Confirm tracking works before launch: analytics events, conversion pixels or server-side events, CRM attribution.
- Respect consent and privacy rules for tracking.
- Define the attribution model and the review cadence (weekly during the campaign).

## 6. Risks
Dependencies, budget overrun, brand or regulatory risk, and the plan if early results are under target (what to cut or double down on).

## Output
A one-page campaign brief (objective, audience, message, channels, budget, timeline, KPIs) followed by the detailed calendar and tracking plan. Mark assumptions and missing data clearly.
