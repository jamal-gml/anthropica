# Marketing Copy

## Brief first
Establish, or ask once for, the essentials:
- **Audience:** who, their problem, awareness stage (unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware).
- **Goal:** the single action wanted (sign up, book a demo, buy, read).
- **Offer and proof:** what is being offered and the evidence (data, testimonials, case studies).
- **Channel and constraints:** format, character limits, brand voice guide, legal or regulatory rules.

## Writing principles
- Lead with the reader's outcome, not the product's features. Features support benefits.
- One message and one call to action per piece. The CTA says exactly what happens ("Start free trial", not "Submit").
- Specific beats vague: numbers, names, timeframes. Cut filler ("innovative", "cutting-edge", "seamless", "world-class").
- Short sentences and paragraphs, scannable headings, active voice.
- Match the brand voice guide. If none exists, state the voice you used.

## Accuracy and compliance
- Every claim must be supportable. Mark unverified stats, prices and quotes as `[VERIFY]` rather than inventing them.
- No fake testimonials, fabricated reviews, false urgency or misleading comparisons.
- Respect regulated-sector rules (financial, health, gambling, alcohol), required disclaimers, and email and privacy law (unsubscribe link, consent).
- Use inclusive language. Avoid idioms that don't translate if the copy will be localised.

## Formats
- **Landing page:** headline (outcome), subhead (how), proof, three to five benefit blocks, objection handling or FAQ, CTA repeated.
- **Email:** subject (≤ 50 chars) plus preview text, one idea, one CTA, plain-text friendly.
- **Ads and social:** hook in the first line, platform character limits respected, two or three variants for testing.

## Output
The copy, plus two or three headline or subject-line variants for A/B testing, the assumed audience and goal, and a list of `[VERIFY]` items.
